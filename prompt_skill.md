## FRAMEWORK:
The skill should be based on a framework. 
I want the framwork to use as much quantitiative methods as possible to assess innovation and the stage of development of technologies.

Before you declare a technology a serious breakthrough candidate, confirm all of the following:
- The field shows novelty, growth, coherence, impact potential, and uncertainty.
- Publication and citation growth are real, not one-off.
- Patent families are rising with credible assignees.
- Patent-to-science linkage is visible.
- University-industry-government interaction is visible.
- A functioning innovation ecosystem exists or is forming.
- Process engineering and production learning have started.
- Public company disclosures show capex, yields, bookings, deployment, or revenue.
- Regulatory/standards conditions are not fatally misaligned.
If most of those boxes are not checked, the technology may still be important, but it is too early to call it a likely near-term industrial breakthrough.

Scientometrics and patent analysis work best together.


## WATCHLIST METHOD: HOW TO GENERATE IDEAS ON WHAT MAY BREAK THROUGH

This SOP does not claim certainty. It gives a disciplined way to create a watchlist.

### BUILD A 5-LAYER DASHBOARD

Input: company name, ticker or SEC.gov CIK number

#### LAYER 1: SCIENCE

- publication growth,
- citation velocity,
- top-lab concentration,
- cross-disciplinary expansion.

#### LAYER 2: IP

- patent family growth,
- assignee quality,
- non-patent literature linkage,
- standards relevance.

#### LAYER 3: INDUSTRIALIZATION

- pilot lines,
- equipment orders,
- process-hiring patterns,
- yield/throughput commentary,
- capex.

#### LAYER 4: ADOPTION

- customer trials,
- design wins,
- procurement commitments,
- ecosystem partnerships.

#### LAYER 5: POLICY AND ECONOMICS

- subsidy/tax-credit alignment,
- export-control effects,
- procurement support,
- carbon-price or reimbursement support,
- labor and infrastructure availability.

### SCORING RULE

Score each layer from 0 to 5.

- 0 to 8: exploratory science.
- 9 to 14: emerging technology worth monitoring.
- 15 to 19: translational momentum.
- 20 to 25: plausible breakthrough or scale-up candidate.

Operational rule: a technology should not enter the highest-priority list unless it scores at least 3 in both Industrialization and Adoption. This avoids confusing scientific excitement with scale readiness.



## RECOMMENDED OPERATING CADENCE

### Monthly

- refresh publication counts,
- refresh citation acceleration,
- refresh patent families and assignees,
- scan patent-office technology dashboards.

### Quarterly

- review public company filings and earnings calls,
- update scorecards for 20 to 50 tracked firms,
- review policy changes and industrial subsidies,
- identify technologies moving from science-led to process-led narratives.

### Every 6 months

- rebuild field maps,
- compare leading universities with leading assignees,
- update breakthrough watchlist,
- remove technologies that remain scientifically interesting but industrially stagnant.


## RED FLAGS

Downgrade confidence if you observe any of the following:

- a field grows rapidly in papers but not in serious patents,
- patenting is dominated by vague claims without technical specificity,
- company disclosures emphasize vision but never yields, installations, or revenue,
- no complementors are appearing,
- the field depends on one celebrity lab or one flagship startup,
- standards/regulatory path is absent,
- manufacturing requirements are extreme and no supplier base is forming.
